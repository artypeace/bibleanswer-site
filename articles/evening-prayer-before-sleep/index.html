Evening prayer

An evening prayer before sleep

Read Matthew 11:28–30 and 1 Peter 5:6–7, then try a short evening prayer when your day is over and your thoughts are still busy.

Bible Answer · November 7, 2026 · 2 min read

An invitation to the tired

Jesus addresses weary people in Matthew 11. In the same passage he speaks about learning from him and taking his yoke. The image is about how we live as his disciples. It can shape an evening prayer without turning it into a formula for falling asleep.

Bring one worry into prayer

First Peter links giving our cares to God with humility. You can name one unfinished matter without pretending it has been solved. If it needs action, decide when you will return to it. For tonight it can wait.

A short prayer before bed

“Jesus, I am tired. Thank you for one good thing I noticed today. You know what I am still carrying. Help me leave what can wait until morning and give me the courage to do what is mine to do.” Take a moment of silence, even if no words come.

What kind of rest does Jesus offer the weary?

In Matthew 11:28–30, Jesus invites weary people to come to him and learn from him. The rest he offers is tied to the character of the teacher who calls them, gentle and humble in heart.
