Getting started

Which Gospel should you read first?

Start with John if you want to explore who Jesus is. Choose Mark for a shorter, fast-moving account, or Luke if you prefer a story that begins with the events around Jesus’ birth.

What Mark, Luke, and John each offer

Mark moves quickly into Jesus’ public ministry, with the call of the first disciples and scenes of teaching and healing in Mark 1. Luke explains why he is writing in Luke 1:1–4 and then tells the events leading up to Jesus’ birth. John opens with the Word and then follows people as they encounter Jesus and respond to him.

Why John is a good place to start

Near the end of the book, John explains why he chose these signs. Read these verses before you begin. They give you a question to carry through the week: what does each scene show about Jesus?

Seven days in the Gospel of John

  1. John 1:1–18. The Word and the light.
  2. John 2:1–11. The wedding at Cana.
  3. John 3:1–17. The conversation with Nicodemus.
  4. John 4:4–26. The woman at the well.
  5. John 9:1–12. A man born blind is healed.
  6. John 11:17–44. Jesus and the family of Lazarus.
  7. John 20:1–31. The resurrection and why John wrote.

Read one passage a day. Write down one thing you noticed and one question. These are selected readings. After the week, you can return to John 1 and read the whole Gospel in order.
